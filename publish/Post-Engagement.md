- Clean up. Remnant scripts and tools should be removed from their corresponding systems and reverting minor configuration changes

# Documentation and Reporting
- Stop notification and timestamp should be sent to client to indicate all of our activity has ceased. Hence, we should have collected all command outputs, screenshots and lists of affected hosts prior.
- A draft document should consist of the following:
	- An attack chain (in the event of full internal compromise or external to internal access) detailing steps taken to achieve compromise
	- A strong executive summary that a non-technical audience can understand
	- Detailed findings specific to the client's environment that include a risk rating, finding impact, remediation recommendations, and high-quality external references related to the issue
	- Adequate steps to reproduce each finding so the team responsible for remediation can understand and test the issue while putting fixes in place
	- Near, medium, and long-term recommendations specific to the environment
	- Appendices which include information such as the target scope, OSINT data (if relevant to the engagement), password cracking analysis (if relevant), discovered ports/services, compromised hosts, compromised accounts, files transferred to client-owned systems, any account creation/system modifications, an Active Directory security analysis (if relevant), relevant scan data/supplementary documentation, and any other information necessary to explain a specific finding or recommendation further

# Report Review

Once the draft report has had time to be circulated within the clients business, we meet to discuss any questions raised by the client. At a bullet-point overview we discuss our perspective and share our experience as it relates to each vulnerability, which may be a conversation held with a senior technician in the company.


# Deliverable acceptance

Our Scope-of-Work should clearly define what our project deliverables must entail to be accepted. Once the Draft report has been reviewed and accepted, we make the further clarifications and return the amended "FINAL" copy to the client.

# Post-Remediation Testing

Often bundled into the cost of a penetration test is a review of any remediations the company has made to their network. We may create a table with each finding, its severity and whether or not it has successfully been remediated.
- As a pentester in this stage, it is important to remember that we are here to audit and not implement actual modifications. This helps us remain integrity to the assessment and not produce any conflicts of interest.

# Data retention

Data collected from our clients network (scans, log outputs, credentials and screenshots) are subject to strict data laws. However, we may keep this for a while as discussed in the Scope of Work/Rules of Engagement document. 

# Close out

Once the test has formally come to a close and we have collected payment, its often worth asking for feedback on the experience, individual testers and the team in general, for our own improvement. A great experience with solid communication and great interactions may later lead to further work with the company.
